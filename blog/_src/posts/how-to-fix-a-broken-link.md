When a visitor clicks a link on your site and lands on a missing page, it creates a frustrating experience and wastes their time. Knowing how to fix a broken link is an essential part of keeping your website healthy, your visitors happy, and your search engine rankings intact. 

Whether you run a small business website or an online store, dead links happen naturally over time as pages move or get deleted. In this guide, I will walk you through how to find these errors and how to fix a broken link step by step.

## Why broken links hurt your website

When a web server cannot find a requested page, it shows a "404 Not Found" error. These errors cause several issues for your business.

First, broken links ruin user experience. If a potential customer clicks on a product recommendation or a contact link and hits a dead end, they are likely to leave your site and go to a competitor instead. 

Second, broken links affect your search visibility. Search engine crawlers like Google use links to discover and index your pages. When a crawler hits a dead link, it wastes its crawling budget and cannot pass valuable "link juice" to the rest of your site. 

> A clean website with working links keeps visitors engaged longer and helps search engines understand your content.

## How to find broken links on your site

Before you can fix any issues, you need a reliable broken link checker to scan your pages. You do not have to check every single page manually.

Here are the most practical ways to find broken links:

- **Google Search Console:** This free tool from Google shows you indexing errors, including pages that return 404 errors. You can learn more about setting it up in my guide on [how to set up Google Search Console](../how-to-set-up-google-search-console/).
- **Online link checkers:** Tools like the Dead Link Checker or Screaming Frog SEO Spider can crawl your domain and spit out a neat list of URLs returning error codes.
- **Platform plugins and apps:** If you use WordPress, plugins like Broken Link Checker can scan your database automatically. For Shopify and Webflow, external crawlers are usually the fastest route.

## Fixing broken links by updating or replacing

Once you have your list of broken links, it is time to fix 404 error website issues at the source. The most direct fix is to update the link itself.

If the broken link is inside your own content (an internal link pointing to a page you deleted or moved), you should edit that page. Open your content editor, find the anchor text pointing to the old URL, and change it to the correct, current URL. 

If the broken link points to an external website that no longer exists, you have two choices. You can either remove the link entirely or replace it with a working link to a similar, up-to-date resource. 

## Using redirects for missing pages

Sometimes a broken link comes from an external site, or from a page you deleted that visitors still try to bookmark and visit. In these cases, editing the link is not enough. You need to set up a redirect.

A redirect tells the browser that the old page has moved to a new address and sends the visitor there automatically instead of showing a 404 error.

Table: Comparing common redirect types for broken links
| Redirect Type | When to Use It | SEO Impact |
|---|---|---|
| 301 Redirect | Permanent move (page deleted or URL changed) | Passes most SEO power to the new page |
| 302 Redirect | Temporary move (few days or weeks) | Keeps SEO power with the original URL |

You can set up redirects easily depending on your platform:
- **WordPress:** You can use plugins like Redirection to map old URLs to new ones in a few clicks.
- **Shopify:** Go to your navigation settings and use the built-in URL redirect feature.
- **Webflow:** Open your site settings, find the SEO tab, and add your redirect rules there.

If you want a deeper dive into this topic, read my guide on [how to redirect URLs properly](../how-to-redirect-urls-properly/).

## Preventing future broken links

Fixing links once is great, but stopping them from happening in the first place saves you hours of cleanup work. 

Adopt these simple habits to keep your link structure clean:

1. **Be careful when changing URLs:** If you must change a page's web address, always set up a 301 redirect from the old URL to the new one immediately.
2. **Test external links:** Before linking to outside sources, make sure the destination page is stable. 
3. **Check after a redesign:** Whenever you migrate a site, change themes, or restructure your shop, run a full site audit right away.

Following these steps protects your [technical SEO for small business](../technical-seo-for-small-business/) efforts and keeps your visitors frustration-free.

## Frequently asked questions {#faq data-toc-label="FAQ"}

### Do broken links hurt my SEO rankings directly?
Google's search engine algorithms do not penalize a site simply for having a few broken links. However, they indirectly hurt your rankings by wasting crawl budget and ruining the user experience, which leads to higher bounce rates.

### Should I redirect every broken link to my homepage?
No. Redirecting irrelevant broken links to your homepage confuses visitors and creates what search engines call a "soft 404" error. Always redirect a broken page to the most relevant replacement page, or let it show a 404 if no relevant page exists.

### How often should I check for broken links?
For small business websites with stable content, checking every three to six months is usually enough. If you run an active e-commerce store with constantly changing products, checking monthly is safer.
