## What is Zapier and how does it work?

Zapier is an online service that connects different web applications so they can share information automatically. Think of it as a digital bridge between tools that normally do not talk to each other, like Google Sheets, Gmail, Slack, and your website.

To understand Zapier, you only need to know three basic terms:

- **Zap:** An automated workflow that connects your apps together.
- **Trigger:** The event that starts the workflow (for example, "a customer fills out a contact form").
- **Action:** What Zapier does automatically after the trigger happens (for example, "send a notification to our team chat").

Every automation follows a straightforward "when this happens, do that" rule. You do not need to write code or understand APIs (the technical connectors software companies use to share data). You just pick the apps you already use, select what should happen, and turn the Zap on.

:::callout Key takeaway
A Zap is simply an "if this, then that" rule running quietly in the background: an event happens in App A, and Zapier automatically carries out a task in App B.
:::

## Step-by-step: How to build your first Zap

If you are following this zapier beginner guide to build your first workflow, start with something simple: saving contact form submissions from your website into a Google Sheet.

Here is how to use Zapier to get this running:

1. **Sign up for an account:** Head to [Zapier](https://zapier.com/) and create a free account.
2. **Click "Create Zap":** You will see an empty canvas with two main blocks: Trigger and Action.
3. **Choose your Trigger app:** Search for your form tool (such as Gravity Forms, WPForms, Webflow, or Typeform). Select the trigger event, which is usually "New Form Submission."
4. **Connect your app account:** Zapier will prompt you to log into your form tool or enter an API key to verify you own the site.
5. **Test the trigger:** Zapier will pull in a recent form submission so you can see real data (names, email addresses, messages). Make sure to submit a test form on your site first if nothing shows up.
6. **Choose your Action app:** Search for **Google Sheets** and set the action event to "Create Spreadsheet Row."
7. **Map your fields:** Select your spreadsheet and the specific sheet tab. Zapier will show each column header you created (such as "Name", "Email", "Date"). Click on each field and choose the corresponding data from your test form.
8. **Test the Zap:** Click "Test step." Check your Google Sheet to verify that the test data appeared in the correct columns.
9. **Publish:** Turn the Zap on. From this point forward, every new form entry will appear in your spreadsheet automatically.

:::callout Tip
Always fill out a realistic test submission on your website before building your Zap. Having clear sample data (like "John Smith" and "john@example.com") makes mapping the fields in Zapier much easier than working with placeholder text.
:::

## Common mistakes to avoid when using Zapier

Zapier is user-friendly, but there are a few traps beginners tend to fall into when they start to automate small business tasks:

- **Automating an unclear process:** If you do not have a consistent routine for handling customer inquiries manually, automating it will only make mistakes happen faster. Map out your process on paper first.
- **Ignoring task allowances:** Zapier's free plan allows a limited number of tasks per month (check their current plans, as limits change). If you set up a high-volume Zap—like recording every page view or spam comment—you can burn through your monthly task allowance in a couple of days.
- **Skipping error alerts:** Make sure your Zapier notification emails are going to an inbox you actually monitor. If an app password changes or an API connection expires, your Zap will pause until you reconnect the account.
- **Building complicated multi-step Zaps too early:** Start with simple two-step Zaps (one trigger, one action). Once you are comfortable with how data flows between apps, you can explore multi-step flows, filters, and delays.

## Frequently asked questions {#faq data-toc-label="FAQ"}

### Is Zapier free to use for a small business?
Zapier offers a free tier that includes basic two-step automations and a limited number of monthly tasks. For multi-step workflows, conditional logic, or higher volume, you will need one of their paid monthly plans (check their site for current pricing).

### Can Zapier connect with WordPress or Shopify stores?
Yes, Zapier has official apps for both WordPress (via WooCommerce and popular form plugins) and Shopify. You can use order creations, inventory changes, or customer sign-ups as triggers to update spreadsheets, send notifications, or sync with your email list.

### What happens if an automation fails to run?
Zapier logs every attempt in your "Zap History" dashboard and shows you the exact error message that caused the failure. On most paid plans, Zapier can automatically retry failed tasks once the issue—such as a temporarily disconnected app—is resolved.
