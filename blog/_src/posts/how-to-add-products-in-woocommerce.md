Learning how to add products in WooCommerce is one of the first practical steps you will take when building an online store with WordPress. Whether you are selling physical goods or digital downloads, getting your online store products set up correctly keeps your inventory accurate and helps customers find what they need. This guide walks you through the entire process, from setting up your dashboard to hitting publish.

## Getting started with your WooCommerce product catalog

Before you create your first listing, it helps to understand where your items live inside your WordPress dashboard. WooCommerce adds a specific menu called "Products" to your sidebar, which acts as the hub for your entire inventory. 

When you click on "Add New" under the Products menu, you open the product editor screen. This screen contains all the fields you need to define what you are selling, how much it costs, and how much stock you have. If you are deciding between platforms, you can review how WordPress compares to other systems in my [WordPress vs Shopify guide](../wordpress-vs-shopify-small-business/), but once you commit to WooCommerce, keeping your catalog organized from day one saves hours of cleanup later.

## How to add a simple physical product

A "simple product" is a standalone item with no variations, such as a coffee mug in a single color or a specific book. This is the most common type of listing you will create during your WooCommerce product setup.

1. Navigate to **Products** > **Add New** in your WordPress dashboard.
2. Type your product name into the main title field at the top.
3. Scroll down to the **Product data** box, which defaults to "Simple product".
4. In the **General** tab, enter your **Regular price** and, optionally, a lower **Sale price** if the item is discounted.
5. Click on the **Inventory** tab to assign a unique Stock Keeping Unit (SKU) code and check the box to "Enable stock management at product level" if you want the store to track quantities automatically.

> Keep your SKUs simple, short, and consistent so you can track sales and restocks without confusion.

## Setting up variable products with options

If you sell an item in multiple sizes, colors, or materials, you need a variable product rather than separate simple products. This setup groups different options under a single page so customers can choose their preferences using dropdown menus.

First, change the dropdown in the **Product data** box from "Simple product" to "Variable product". Next, go to the **Attributes** tab to define your options. For example, you might create an attribute called "Size" and type in values separated by vertical bars, like `Small | Medium | Large`. 

Check the box that says "Used for variations", then click the **Variations** tab. Here, you can click "Create variations from all attributes" to let WooCommerce automatically generate every possible combination. You must set a price for each variation individually, or the item cannot be purchased. ## Writing product descriptions that work

A product page needs two different text areas: the main long description and the short description. Putting the right details in each place helps customers make buying decisions quickly.

The long description sits further down the page and should cover features, dimensions, materials, and care instructions. The short description appears right next to the product title and price near the top of the page. Use bullet points in your short description to highlight key benefits at a glance. For more tips on crafting persuasive text, read my guide on [how to write product descriptions that sell](../how-to-write-product-descriptions/).

:::tip Keep text scannable
Most online shoppers scan rather than read every word. Use short paragraphs, clear headings, and bullet points to break up technical details.
:::

## Adding images and organizing categories

Visuals drive online sales, so adding clear photos is a vital part of effective online store management. On the right side of the product editor, you will find two image panels.

- **Product image:** The main photo that appears in your shop catalog and on the product page.
- **Product gallery:** Additional photos showing different angles, close-ups, or the item in use.

Below the images, you will find your **Product categories** and **Product tags**. Categories act like aisles in a supermarket (for example, "Apparel" or "Accessories"), while tags are specific keywords that describe smaller features. Checking the right category ensures your items appear in the correct navigation menus. ## Publishing and checking your product page

Once you have filled out your titles, prices, descriptions, and images, look toward the top right corner of the screen at the **Publish** meta box. You can click "Preview" to see how the page looks to visitors before it goes live.

If you are not ready for the public to see the item, click "Edit" next to Status and change it to "Draft". Otherwise, click the blue **Publish** button. After publishing, click the permalink link near the top to view the live page on your storefront and test the "Add to cart" button yourself. ## Frequently asked questions {#faq data-toc-label="FAQ"}

### What is the difference between a simple product and a variable product in WooCommerce?
A simple product is a single, standalone item with one fixed price and inventory count. A variable product allows you to offer choices like different sizes or colors on the same page, where each option can have its own price and stock level.

### Do I need to enter a SKU for every product?
While WooCommerce does not strictly require a SKU to publish a product, using one is strongly recommended. SKUs make tracking inventory, processing orders, and managing stock levels much easier as your catalog grows.

### How many product images should I add?
You should always include at least one clear main product image with a plain background. Adding two to four gallery images showing different angles, packaging, or the item in use helps build buyer confidence.
