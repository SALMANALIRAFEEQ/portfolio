## Why your small business needs Google Analytics 4

Google Analytics 4 is the current standard platform provided by Google to track website visitors. Unlike older versions of analytics, GA4 focuses heavily on user events and interactions rather than just page views, making it much more useful for modern websites and online stores.

Using analytics for small business websites helps you answer simple questions like:
- How many people visit my site each week?
- Which pages or products do they look at the most?
- Are visitors finding me through search engines, social media, or direct links?
- Which city or country do my potential customers live in?

When you combine this tracking data with good search engine optimization practices—such as those outlined in my [on-page SEO checklist for small business websites](../on-page-seo-checklist/)—you can see clearly which pages bring in organic search traffic and which ones need more work.

> If you cannot measure how people find and use your website, you cannot reliably improve it.

## How to create your Google Analytics account

Before you touch any code on your website, you need to set up your account on the official analytics platform. This process takes only a few minutes.

1. Go to Google and sign in with your primary business Google account, then navigate to the main Google Analytics homepage.
2. Click the button to start measuring and enter your business name as the account name.
3. Set your data sharing preferences (leaving the default boxes checked is usually fine).
4. Create a property name, which is typically your business or website name, and select your local time zone and currency.
5. Fill in your business details such as industry category and company size, then accept the terms of service.

Once your property is created, you will be prompted to choose a platform to set up data collection. Choose the "Web" option since you are tracking a website or online store.

## How to connect GA4 to your website or online store

After creating your web property, Google will generate a measurement ID (which looks like `G-XXXXXXXXXX`) and provide a snippet of tracking code. How you add this to your site depends entirely on the platform you use.

If you use Shopify, you do not need to paste raw code into your theme files. You can simply go to your online store settings, look for the Google channels or preferences area, and paste your measurement ID or connect your Google account directly. For more platform-specific tips, you can read my guide on [WordPress vs Shopify for small business](../wordpress-vs-shopify-small-business/).

If you use WordPress, the easiest method is to install a trusted plugin or your SEO tool of choice, which will have a designated field for your GA4 measurement ID. Avoid pasting raw code into your theme files unless you are comfortable doing so, as updates can sometimes wipe out manual edits. 

:::tip
Always test your installation after connecting. Open your website in a private browsing window, go to your GA4 real-time report, and check if your visit shows up as an active user.
:::

## Important settings to check after installation

Getting the tracking code on your pages is only the first step. To ensure your data remains clean and useful, you should adjust a few default settings right after your analytics setup.

First, turn off or limit internal traffic filtering if you do not want your own office visits and your developer's tests skewing your numbers. You can define your own IP address under the data stream settings so that visits from your team are ignored in standard reports.

Second, check your data retention settings. By default, Google holds onto detailed event data for only two months, but you can change this setting in the admin panel to fourteen months so you can compare yearly trends.

Finally, link your Google Analytics property to Google Search Console if you use it. This connection brings organic search queries directly into your analytics interface, helping you understand which keywords bring visitors to your pages. 

## How to view your basic visitor reports

When you first log in to your dashboard, the interface can feel overwhelming because it includes dozens of advanced menus. As a small business owner, you only need to focus on a few key reports to track website visitors effectively.

- **Realtime:** Shows you who is on your site right now and which pages they are viewing. This is great for testing new changes.
- **Acquisition (Traffic acquisition):** Shows where your visitors came from, such as organic search, social media, paid ads, or referral links.
- **Engagement (Pages and screens):** Shows which specific pages on your site get the most views and how long people stay on them.
- **User attributes:** Shows basic demographic details like country, language, and the type of device (mobile versus desktop) your visitors use.

If you run an e-commerce store, you will also want to configure conversion events to track purchases, form submissions, and newsletter signups. Tracking these actions helps you see whether your marketing efforts are actually generating revenue.

## Frequently asked questions {#faq data-toc-label="FAQ"}

### Is Google Analytics 4 completely free to use?
Yes, the standard version of Google Analytics 4 is free for almost all small and medium-sized business websites. There is a paid enterprise version called Analytics 360, but you will only need that if your site handles massive global traffic volumes.

### Why is my real-time data not showing up immediately?
Data can sometimes take a few minutes to appear in your real-time report after you first install the code. If it still does not show up, check your browser extensions, as ad blockers often block Google Analytics tracking scripts from loading.

### Do I need to know how to code to set up GA4?
No. Most popular content management systems and e-commerce platforms offer simple integration fields where you just paste your measurement ID, eliminating the need to edit website code directly.
