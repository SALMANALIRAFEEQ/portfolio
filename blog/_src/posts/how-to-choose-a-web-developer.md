Finding the right web developer for small business projects can feel overwhelming, especially if technical terms sound like a foreign language. Knowing how to choose a web developer saves you time, money, and frustration down the road. Whether you are building your first site or updating an old one, a structured approach helps you hire a web developer who fits your goals.

## Determine what you actually need before you start looking

Before you start looking at portfolios or finding a web designer, write down what your website needs to do. A local service business that just needs a contact form and a few service pages requires a very different approach than an online store with hundreds of products. 

Make a simple list of your core requirements.
- **Site type:** Do you need a brochure site, a portfolio, or an online store?
- **Platform preference:** Do you already know if you want to use [WordPress](https://wordpress.org/), [Shopify](https://www.shopify.com/), or Webflow?
- **Content:** Do you have your text and images ready, or do you need help writing and organizing them?
- **Ongoing needs:** Will you update the site yourself, or do you need someone to handle monthly maintenance?

If you are stuck between platforms, it helps to read up on options like [Shopify vs Webflow](../shopify-vs-webflow/) or [WordPress vs Shopify: Which Is Better for Your Small Online Store?](../wordpress-vs-shopify-small-business/) before talking to anyone.

## Check their platform experience and past work

When you want to hire a web developer, look closely at their past work. You want someone who has built sites similar to the one you need. If you are launching an online store, a developer who mostly builds static portfolio sites might not know how to set up payment gateways and tax rates properly.

Ask to see live websites they have built, not just screenshots. Click through those sites on your phone to see how they look on mobile screens.

## Ask the right questions about maintenance and ownership

Building a website is only the first step. You need to know what happens after the site launches. Many business owners make the mistake of hiring someone without clarifying who owns the domain, hosting accounts, and site files. 

When you have your initial chat, ask a few direct questions to clarify ownership and future support.
- Do I own my domain name, hosting account, and all site files completely?
- What happens if I want to make small text changes myself later?
- Do you offer training so my staff can manage daily tasks like products or blog posts?
- How do you handle site backups and security updates?

:::callout Tip
Always ensure that your domain name and main hosting accounts are registered in your own name and credit card, not the developer's. You should be the main owner with full admin access from day one.
:::

If you choose a WordPress site, you should also discuss basic security and speed habits, such as those covered in [How to Secure a WordPress Site: A Practical Beginner's Guide](../how-to-secure-a-wordpress-site/) and [How to Speed Up a WordPress Site: A Practical Guide](../how-to-speed-up-a-wordpress-site/).

## Understand communication and project timelines

A talented developer who never replies to messages can cause massive delays for your business. Before signing any agreement, find out how they communicate and how long the project will take.

Ask how they prefer to share updates—whether through email, project management boards, or messaging apps. Clarify how many rounds of revisions are included in their quote so there are no surprises when you want to tweak a design element. 

Clear timelines protect both you and the developer. Ask for a realistic schedule that outlines key milestones, such as when the initial design draft is ready, when you need to provide feedback, and when the final testing phase begins.

## Beware of red flags when hiring a developer

Spotting warning signs early saves you from costly mistakes. If a deal sounds too good to be true, it usually is. 

Watch out for these common warning signs:
- **Vague pricing:** If a developer refuses to give a clear breakdown of costs or demands full payment upfront before showing any plan, walk away.
- **No portfolio:** If they claim to have years of experience but cannot show a single live website they built, be cautious.
- **Guaranteed number one rankings:** No legitimate developer can guarantee your site will rank first on search engines instantly. Be wary of anyone promising instant SEO miracles.
- **Lock-in tactics:** If they refuse to give you admin access or try to trap you into expensive, mandatory hosting plans that you cannot control, look elsewhere.

A trustworthy developer is transparent about their skills, honest about timelines, and happy to explain technical concepts in plain English.

## Frequently asked questions {#faq data-toc-label="FAQ"}

### How much should a small business website cost?
Costs vary widely depending on the platform, number of pages, and custom features you need. Always check the current price and get a detailed quote that specifies what is included before starting.

### Should I hire a freelancer or an agency?
Freelancers are usually more affordable and offer direct, personal communication. Agencies offer a wider team of specialists, but often come with higher overhead costs.

### Do I need to keep paying a developer after the site launches?
Not necessarily, but basic maintenance like security updates, backups, and minor updates are necessary. You can either learn to do these routine tasks yourself or hire a developer on a monthly retainer to handle them.
