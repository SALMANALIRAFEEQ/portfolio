## Why standard product descriptions fail to sell

Most online store owners make the mistake of copying and pasting the manufacturer’s technical specifications directly onto their website. While measurements, materials, and weight matter, dry data does not motivate a reader to click the buy button. 

When a shopper visits your product page, they are asking one main question: "What is in this for me?" If your text only lists factory details, the reader has to do the mental work of figuring out how your item solves their problem. Most people will not bother. They will simply close the tab and buy from a competitor who makes the benefits crystal clear.

## Focus on customer benefits instead of basic features

The easiest way to improve your ecommerce copywriting is to turn every feature into a customer benefit. A feature is what the product is or has, while a benefit is what the customer actually experiences because of it.

Look at your product catalog and translate your technical specifications into emotional or practical advantages:

Table: Transforming features into customer benefits
| Feature (What it is) | Benefit (What it does for the buyer) |
|---|---|
| Fully waterproof nylon fabric | Keeps your gear completely dry during sudden downpours |
| Weighs only 250 grams | Won't weigh your shoulders down during long hikes |
| Built-in cable management clips | Keeps your desk tidy and stops tangled cords |

When you emphasize how an item makes life easier, faster, or more enjoyable, your product page writing instantly becomes more persuasive. Speak directly to the reader using the word "you" to make the text feel personal.

:::callout Tip
Always ask yourself "So what?" after writing a feature. If your description says the jacket has sealed zippers, ask "So what?" The answer is that no rain will leak through your pockets, which becomes your customer benefit.
:::

## Structure your text for quick reading

Online shoppers rarely read every single word on a product page. Instead, they scan the screen looking for key details like price, size, color, and shipping terms. If your description is one giant block of dense text, readers will miss your best selling points.

Break up your text so that it is effortless to scan in just a few seconds:
- Use short paragraphs that are no more than three sentences long.
- Highlight crucial details using bullet points for quick reading.
- Bold a few key phrases to guide the reader's eye down the page.
- Keep your tone conversational, direct, and honest about what the item does.

If you sell physical goods that require careful tracking, clear descriptions should also match your actual stock reality. ## Use AI tools to draft descriptions faster

Writing unique descriptions for dozens or hundreds of items can take weeks. This is where modern artificial intelligence tools can save you a massive amount of time on content work. 

You do not need to write every sentence from scratch. You can feed your basic product details into an AI assistant and ask it to generate a draft. 

```text
Prompt example:
Write a short, engaging product description for a stainless steel travel mug. 
Highlight that it keeps coffee hot for 12 hours, fits in car cup holders, 
and never leaks. Use a friendly and direct tone.
```

## Optimize your text for search engines

Great product descriptions do not just persuade humans; they also help search engines understand what you are selling. Writing clear text naturally incorporates the search terms your potential buyers are typing into Google.

Before you publish a product page, make sure you cover the basics:
1. Include your main product name and key descriptive words naturally in the opening paragraph.
2. Write a descriptive meta title and meta description for search engine results pages.
3. Use descriptive file names and alt text for all your product images so search engines can read them.

## Frequently asked questions {#faq data-toc-label="FAQ"}

### How long should an online product description be?
The length depends on what you are selling. Simple items like plain t-shirts might only need a short paragraph and a few bullet points, while complex electronics or high-ticket items require more detail to answer buyer questions and build trust.

### Should I copy product descriptions from the manufacturer?
You should avoid copying manufacturer text because hundreds of other stores use the exact same wording. Search engines penalize duplicate content, and identical descriptions give shoppers no reason to buy from your store instead of a competitor.

### How do I write descriptions when I have hundreds of products?
Start by focusing on your top twenty bestselling items and rewrite those manually to maximize your revenue. For the rest of your catalog, use AI tools to generate bulk drafts quickly, then spend time reviewing and polishing them.
