Many business owners overlook the descriptive text behind their site graphics, yet learning how to write alt text for images is a simple way to help search engines understand your pages. When you know how to write alt text for images correctly, you make your site easier for screen readers to navigate while giving your on-page SEO a steady boost.

In this guide, you will learn what to write, which mistakes to avoid, and how to update your images across popular platforms.

## What is image alt text and why does it matter?

Alt text (alternative text) is a short written description of an image added to the HTML code of a web page. If an image fails to load, or if a visitor is using a screen reader because of a visual impairment, this text is displayed or read aloud instead. 

For search engines like Google, alt text acts as a translation tool. Because crawlers read text much better than they "see" pixels, image alt text seo helps search engines figure out what is inside a photo, graphic, or product shot. This helps your pages rank for relevant searches and brings in organic traffic. For a broader overview of how to structure your pages, you can check out my [on-page seo checklist](../on-page-seo-checklist/).

## How to write clear and useful alt text

Writing good descriptions does not require complicated copywriting. The goal is simply to describe the image as if you were explaining it to someone over the phone.

- **Be specific:** Describe what is actually visible in the photo rather than making vague guesses.
- **Keep it concise:** Aim for a single sentence, usually around ten to fifteen words.
- **Include context:** If the image shows a specific product or location relevant to your business, mention it naturally.

> Write descriptions for human readers first, and let search engines pick up the context naturally.

For online stores, this practice ties closely into how you [optimize product pages for seo](../optimize-product-pages-for-seo/). If a customer is shopping for a blue leather jacket, the alt text should say "Blue leather biker jacket with silver zippers" rather than just "jacket".

## Common mistakes to avoid when writing alt text

It is easy to fall into bad habits when updating dozens of images on a busy website. Avoiding these common errors will keep your site compliant and user-friendly.

| Mistake | Why it hurts | What to do instead |
|---|---|---|
| Stuffing keywords | Looks like spam to search engines and confuses screen readers | Describe the image naturally and use your keyword only if it fits |
| Using "image of" or "photo of" | Wastes valuable character space on redundant words | Jump straight into the subject of the photo |
| Leaving alt tags empty | Blocks screen reader users and misses ranking opportunities | Add a brief description to every meaningful graphic |
| Copying the same text | Tells search engines all images are identical | Write a unique description for every distinct graphic |

:::callout Tip
Decorative images—like background shapes, divider lines, or spacing icons—do not need alt text. Leave the field blank so screen readers skip them entirely.
:::

## Adding alt text in WordPress, Shopify, and Webflow

No matter which platform powers your website, updating your descriptions takes just a few clicks. Here is how to handle website accessibility basics and image optimization across the three main platforms:

- **WordPress:** Click on any image inside the block editor or open your Media Library. On the right-hand panel, locate the "Alt Text" field and type your description before saving or inserting the image.
- **Shopify:** Open your product page or theme editor, click on an image, and look for the "Add alt text" or "Update alt text" link next to the thumbnail.
- **Webflow:** Click on the image on your canvas, open the Element Settings panel on the right, and type your description into the "Alt Text" input box under the image settings.

## Testing your website for missing alt text

Once you have updated your key pages, you need to verify that nothing was missed. You can check your work using a few straightforward methods.

1. Right-click anywhere on your live web page and select "Inspect" or "Inspect Element" to open the developer tools.
2. Click the element selector tool (usually a small icon of a mouse and square) and click on any image on the page.
3. Look at the HTML code highlight in the elements panel to confirm that `alt="..."` contains your description.
4. Run a general site audit using browser extensions or accessibility checkers to flag any images that still lack descriptions.

## Frequently asked questions {#faq data-toc-label="FAQ"}

### Do I need to include my main keyword in every image alt text?
No. You should only include your keyword if it accurately describes what is in the specific image. Forcing keywords where they do not belong harms your user experience and does not help your rankings.

### What should I write for my company logo alt text?
Your logo alt text should simply state your company name followed by the word "logo", such as "Salman Ali Rafeeq logo". This helps identify the brand if the image fails to load and serves as a home page link anchor for screen readers.

### Are there images that do not need alt text?
Yes. Purely decorative images, such as background patterns, decorative borders, or spacing icons, should have empty alt attributes so screen readers ignore them and focus on meaningful content.
