If you are about to open an online store, the first big decision is the platform. For most small businesses it comes down to two names: **WordPress (with WooCommerce)** and **Shopify**. Both are excellent, both power millions of stores, and both can sell your products well. The right choice depends on your budget, how hands-on you want to be, and where you want the business to go.

I build stores on both platforms, so here is an honest comparison without the sales pitch.

## The short answer {#short-answer}

Choose **Shopify** if you want to start selling quickly and never think about hosting, updates or security. Choose **WordPress with WooCommerce** if you want full control, lower running costs over time, and a site that can grow into much more than a shop.

## What each platform is {#what-they-are}

### WordPress + WooCommerce

WordPress is free, open-source software that runs a large share of the web. On its own it is a website builder; add the free [WooCommerce](https://woocommerce.com/) plugin and it becomes a full online store. You rent your own hosting, install it, and you own everything: the code, the data and the design.

### Shopify

[Shopify](https://www.shopify.com/) is a hosted, all-in-one service. You pay a monthly subscription and Shopify takes care of hosting, security, updates and checkout. You customise the look with themes and add features with apps from its app store.

## Side-by-side comparison {#comparison}

| Factor | WordPress + WooCommerce | Shopify |
|---|---|---|
| Setup speed | Slower, needs hosting and setup | Fast, sign up and start |
| Running cost | Hosting + optional paid plugins | Monthly plan + app fees |
| Maintenance | You (or your developer) handle updates | Handled by Shopify |
| Design freedom | Unlimited | High, within theme limits |
| Content and blogging | Best in class | Basic |
| Ownership | You own the site and data | Hosted on Shopify’s platform |

## Cost: what you really pay {#cost}

WordPress itself is free, but a store still costs money to run. Shopify’s pricing is simpler but adds up as you grow. The real costs usually look like this:

- **WordPress:** domain, hosting, a premium theme (optional) and any paid plugins you choose.
- **Shopify:** domain, a monthly plan, paid apps for extra features, and possibly transaction fees depending on your payment setup.

For a small catalogue, the totals are often close in the first year. The difference shows later: WordPress costs stay fairly flat, while Shopify costs tend to rise with every app you add.

## Ease of use and day-to-day running {#ease-of-use}

Shopify wins on simplicity. Adding products, managing orders and processing payments all happen in one clean dashboard, and there is nothing to update or back up.

WordPress has a learning curve, but once it is set up properly the daily work is just as easy. The difference is responsibility: someone needs to keep plugins updated and the site backed up. With a good developer or a maintenance plan, that is a non-issue.

> The best platform is the one your team will actually keep updated. A neglected store on either platform will lose sales.

## SEO and growth {#seo}

Both platforms can rank well on Google. WordPress gives you more control over every SEO detail: URLs, page structure, schema markup and especially content, because it started life as a blogging platform. Shopify covers the essentials out of the box but is stricter about URL structure.

Whichever you choose, the basics matter more than the platform: fast pages, clear product titles, unique descriptions and a proper meta description on every page, like this:

```html
<meta name="description" content="Handmade seed pencils that grow into plants. Free delivery across Pakistan.">
```

Clean, readable URLs such as `/products/seed-pencils` also help both shoppers and search engines.

## Which one should you choose? {#which-to-choose}

### Pick WordPress + WooCommerce if…

- You want full ownership and no platform lock-in.
- Content, blogging and SEO are part of your growth plan.
- You need custom features or an unusual design.

### Pick Shopify if…

- You want to launch fast with minimal technical work.
- You prefer one predictable monthly bill over managing hosting.
- Your focus is selling products, not publishing content.

:::callout Key takeaway
Starting small and want to sell this month? Go with Shopify. Planning to build a brand with content, custom features and lower long-term costs? Go with WordPress and WooCommerce. You can see real stores I have built on both in [my work](../../#work).
:::

## Frequently asked questions {#faq}

### Can I move from Shopify to WordPress later?

Yes. Products, customers and orders can be migrated, though it takes careful planning to keep your URLs and search rankings intact.

### Which is better for a store in Pakistan?

Both work well. The bigger question is which local payment and delivery options you need, so check that your chosen platform supports them before you commit.

### Do I need a developer?

For a basic Shopify store, not necessarily. For a polished WordPress store, or any store you want to grow seriously, a developer will save you time and costly mistakes.
